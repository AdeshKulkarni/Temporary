I completely understand why this is incredibly frustrating. You are doing exactly what you are supposed to do, but your company's network keeps rejecting it.
To be completely transparent: this is not a code issue, and it is not a Maven issue. A 403 Forbidden error means your company’s internal security firewall (JFrog Artifactory) is actively blocking you from downloading these files. Your DevSecOps team has locked down the repository, and they haven't approved the secure versions of this specific library yet.
However, since we cannot bypass your company's firewall to download the secure version of Derby, we will just delete Derby entirely.
Apache Derby is just an embedded, lightweight database. Hadoop and Hive pull it in by default for local testing, but since your pom.xml shows you are using an actual Oracle database (ojdbc11), your application does not need Derby to function.
Here is how to rip it out completely so JFrog stops looking for it:
Step 1: Remove the Derby Override
Go to your <dependencyManagement> block and delete the Derby entry we added earlier. Remove these lines completely:
            <!-- 7. Derby -->
            <dependency>
                <groupId>org.apache.derby</groupId>
                <artifactId>derby</artifactId>
                <version>10.15.2.1</version> 
            </dependency>

Step 2: Exclude Derby from Hive
Scroll down to your <dependencies> block, find your hive-jdbc dependency, and add Derby to the <exclusions> list alongside the others:
        <dependency>
            <groupId>org.apache.hive</groupId>
            <artifactId>hive-jdbc</artifactId>
            <version>3.1.3</version>
            <exclusions>
                <!-- Existing exclusions -->
                <exclusion>
                    <groupId>org.slf4j</groupId>
                    <artifactId>slf4j-log4j12</artifactId>
                </exclusion>
                <!-- ... other exclusions ... -->
                
                <!-- NEW: Rip out the blocked Derby database completely -->
                <exclusion>
                    <groupId>org.apache.derby</groupId>
                    <artifactId>derby</artifactId>
                </exclusion>
            </exclusions>
        </dependency>

Run mvn clean install one more time. Because we have explicitly told Maven to throw Derby in the trash instead of downloading it, it will completely bypass the 403 Forbidden firewall block.
