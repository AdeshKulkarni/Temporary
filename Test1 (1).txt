I know exactly what is happening here! You are experiencing the classic "UTC to IST Time Shift" bug.
Your Indian Standard Time (IST) is exactly 5.5 hours ahead of UTC.
If a record was created at 4:00 PM (IST), it will show up on your screen as 10:30 AM (UTC). This perfectly matches your description of the time being "before four/five hours" and showing "AM instead of PM".
Even though you brilliantly added TimeZone.setDefault(TimeZone.getTimeZone("Asia/Kolkata")); in your Java VoucherServiceApplication.java, there are two separate systems that secretly ignore that setting.
Here are the exact 2 places you need to manually change to fix this permanently.
STEP 1: Fix the Spring Boot JSON Converter
When your Java backend sends the data to your UI, it uses a tool called Jackson to convert Java to JSON. Jackson ignores your TimeZone.setDefault and uses UTC by default, which strips your 5.5 hours away right before sending it to the frontend!
What to do:
Open your application.properties (or application.yml) file and add this exact line at the bottom:
# Add this to force the JSON response to stay in Indian Time
spring.jackson.time-zone=Asia/Kolkata

STEP 2: Fix the Oracle PL/SQL Procedure
In your PROCESS_JOURNAL_BATCH procedure, you are using CURRENT_TIMESTAMP.
The Bug: CURRENT_TIMESTAMP relies on the JDBC Connection Session. When Java connects to Oracle, the JDBC driver sometimes secretly sets the session timezone to UTC.
The Fix: We must use SYSTIMESTAMP instead. SYSTIMESTAMP looks directly at the physical database server clock and completely ignores the Java JDBC session, making the calculation bulletproof.
You need to replace CURRENT_TIMESTAMP with SYSTIMESTAMP in 3 exact places in your procedure:
1. In the GL_TRANSACTIONS Insert (Around Line 64):
-- FIND THIS LINE:
CAST(CURRENT_TIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) AS POST_DATE,      

-- REPLACE WITH THIS:
CAST(SYSTIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) AS POST_DATE, 

2. In the VOUCHER_TRANSACTIONS Insert (Around Line 96):
-- FIND THIS LINE:
CAST(CURRENT_TIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) AS POST_DATE,      

-- REPLACE WITH THIS:
CAST(SYSTIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) AS POST_DATE, 

3. In the Finalize Batch Status Update (Around Line 159):
-- FIND THIS LINE:
EXECUTION_DATE =  CAST(CURRENT_TIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) 

-- REPLACE WITH THIS:
EXECUTION_DATE =  CAST(SYSTIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata' AS TIMESTAMP) 

Why this works:
 * By changing to SYSTIMESTAMP in Oracle, the database will save the exact local Indian time, regardless of what the JDBC driver thinks.
 * By adding spring.jackson.time-zone=Asia/Kolkata to your properties file, Spring Boot will stop converting your perfectly saved Indian Time back into UTC when it sends it to the UI.
Recompile your procedure with these 3 changes and restart your Spring Boot app, and the UI will instantly show the correct PM timings!
